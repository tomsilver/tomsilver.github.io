<b>Contact</b>: tsilver@princeton.edu

I am an assistant professor at Princeton in the <a href="https://ece.princeton.edu/people/tsilver">Electrical and Computer Engineering</a> department and a <a href="https://robo.princeton.edu/">core faculty member in robotics</a>. I am also associated with the <a href="https://csml.princeton.edu/">Center for Statistics and Machine Learning</a>. I completed my PhD in May 2024 at MIT EECS, where I was advised by <a href="https://lis.csail.mit.edu/">Leslie Kaelbling</a> and <a href="https://cocosci.mit.edu/">Josh Tenenbaum</a>. I was a postdoc with <a href="https://emprise.cs.cornell.edu/">Tapo Bhattacharjee</a> at Cornell in 2024-2025. I received my B.A. from Harvard with highest honors in computer science and mathematics in 2016.

I direct the <a href="https://prpl-group.com"><b style="color: #b509ac">Princeton Robot Planning and Learning (PRPL)</b> lab</a>. Our mission is to develop generalist robots that plan and learn to help people. Most of our work is at the intersection of automated planning and machine learning: learning to plan and planning to learn while making efficient use of limited data and time. We often use techniques from <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01083">task and motion planning</a>, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06317">program synthesis</a>, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11014">foundation models</a>, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06298">reinforcement learning</a>, and <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.10680">neuro-symbolic ML</a>. But we are driven by <a href="/blog/2024/lessons/">problems, not methods</a>, and we are motivated to solve general problems in applications like <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14968">robot caregiving</a> where robots can empower people to remain independent.
